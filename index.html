Exile & Sovereignty

A village with no country

I am Tenzin Palgyal. I was born into the Tibetan diaspora in India. When you grow up in exile, the notion of "homeland" is not defined by borders on a map. It lives in the memory of our elders, the script carved into prayer stones, and the collective duty to keep our heritage alive.

At the Central Tibetan Administration's Department of Home in Dharamshala, my daily work bridges that generational memory with modern digital infrastructure. When I write a database migration in PostgreSQL or develop a portal in React and Laravel, I am not merely shipping code. I am safeguarding the records of 40+ settlements, preserving land documents, and equipping our community to thrive in the modern age.

That same commitment drove me to create the Tibetan Keyboard typing tutor and now lead the architecture of our Digital Archive. Code is our quiet resistance. Technology is our bridge to the future.

Frequently asked questions

Key context on my technical background, current institutional work, and collaboration readiness.

What is your core role at the Department of Home?

I lead full-stack web development and institutional IT operations. My responsibilities span architecting our official web portal, building the MS Project internal workflow suite, managing database security in PostgreSQL, and maintaining reliable server infrastructure.

What technology stack do you rely on for major builds?

My primary production stack consists of React for responsive client interfaces, Laravel (PHP) for robust API backends, and PostgreSQL for relational data integrity. I also utilize Figma for design systems and WordPress for content-heavy institutional publishing.

Why did you develop the Tibetan Keyboard typing tool?

Tibetan script features complex character stacking with 30 root letters, 4 vowels, and subjoined ligatures. Diaspora youth and researchers struggled to find intuitive digital typing tutors, so I built a zero-dependency interactive web tutor to teach typing fluency directly in the browser.

What is the status of the Digital Archive project?

The Digital Archive is currently in active development. It is engineered to digitize, catalog, and securely store decades of historical refugee resettlement records, elder oral narratives, and official decrees with dual Tibetan and English metadata search.

What does "a village with no country" represent?

It represents our lived reality as Tibetan refugees born in diaspora. Without an internationally recognized passport country, our villages and communities are built on shared culture, resilience, and memory. In software engineering, this manifests as building resilient digital infrastructure that has no geographic boundary.

Are you open to collaborative projects or remote engineering?

Yes. While my core mission is serving the Central Tibetan Administration, I am always keen to collaborate on high-impact civic technology, linguistic preservation tools, open-source utilities, and modern web applications.
